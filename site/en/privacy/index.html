RideScreen for iOS — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

RideScreen is an iPhone app that shows your motorcycle's dashboard picture (speed, engine data, map and navigation) on the bike's own screen and lets you record trips. It is published by Manel García (GitHub: mgb1982), as an individual. Contact: hola@ridescreen.app

In short

  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking. The app never asks you to sign up and does not use data for advertising.
  • Your data stays on your iPhone. Trips, routes, settings and bike data are stored on the phone.
  • The app does not sell or share your data with third parties.
  • The few internet connections the app makes are listed below.

Permissions and what they are for

PermissionPurpose
Location (also in the background, while you ride or the app is connected to the dash)Record trips, compute distance and speed, guide you along a route, show the map on the dash and remember where you parked.
BluetoothRead the battery and control your bike's PKE box, and read engine data from an OBD adapter, only when you ask.
Local networkConnect to the bike screen's Wi‑Fi to send it the picture, and to a nearby iPhone in Passenger mode.
CameraScan the QR code on the bike screen and photograph where you parked.
Motion sensorsDetect a possible fall while you record a trip, only if you turn on fall detection.
Photos (add only)Save the trip video you create to Photos. The app does not read your photo library. The dash background picture is chosen with Apple's picker and stays on your phone.

You can withdraw any permission in iOS Settings; the features that depend on it will stop working.

Data stored on your iPhone

Bike profiles (the dash Wi‑Fi name and password), trips and routes (GPS points, distance, times, speeds), parking spots, settings, the background picture you pick and a technical diagnostic log. None of it is sent to a server. It only leaves the phone if you share it: for example when you tap "Send report", export a GPX or save a video. You can delete it from the app or by uninstalling.

Internet connections

  • Maps. The dash and trip maps use Apple Maps (MapKit). Apple receives the map requests under its own privacy policy.
  • Traffic lights. To warn you about traffic lights on a route, the app queries Overpass (OpenStreetMap data) for the area you are riding through.
  • Fuel stations. It downloads small files of nearby fuel stations from ridescreen.app.
  • Purchase. The one-time unlock is handled by Apple's App Store. The app never sees your payment details.

Optional features that use a server

These two features are off until you turn them on. They use a RideScreen service hosted on Cloudflare, and there are no accounts.

  • Anonymous usage and crash reports (Settings › Telemetry). If you turn them on, the app sends a random installation identifier, the app version, the major iOS version and the language, once a day. Only if you tap "Report a problem" is a technical text with personal data removed sent as well. The service does not store your IP address or country, and deletes the data after 180 days. You can turn them off at any time.
  • Live route sharing (Routes tab). It only starts when you start it. It sends the name you choose, latitude, longitude, speed and time every few seconds, so whoever has the link can see you on a map. It ends when you stop it or shortly after the ride ends (12 hours at most), and the service deletes the positions 24 hours after it ends.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not collect their data.
